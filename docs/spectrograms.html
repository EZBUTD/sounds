Seeing sounds

A spectrogram is a picture of sound: time runs left to right, frequency runs bottom to top, and darker means more energy at that frequency and moment.

Two allophones of one phoneme, visualized

The puff of air you can see

Both words below store the same phoneme, /t/ — but the mouth produces a different sound in each. Those two sounds are allophones of /t/: alternative pronunciations that never distinguish two English words in these contexts. English organizes them as one t category, and many speakers do not notice the difference until it is pointed out.

The notation is the quickest way to keep the two levels apart. /slashes/ mark a phoneme, the category the language stores. [brackets] mark an allophone, what a mouth actually did. Each card below reads /t/ → [tʰ]: phoneme → allophone

In top the release of the [tʰ] is followed by a stretch of noisy, high-frequency energy — the aspiration — before the vowel starts. In stop that stretch is almost absent, because a preceding /s/ suppresses it, giving plain [t]. The gap between the release and the start of voicing is called voice onset time, and it is the standard way phoneticians measure aspiration. It is the main measurement used here to compare these two tokens, alongside the visible release and aspiration noise.

Press play and a cursor tracks across the image in time with the audio, so you can hear which part of the picture you are looking at — the aspiration is the shaded stretch the cursor crosses between the two markers.

Your own voice

A rough acoustic estimate of your vowel

A vowel's first resonance (F1) broadly tracks vowel height (and therefore mouth opening), while the second (F2) broadly tracks tongue frontness; lip rounding and vocal-tract shape also affect them. A simple estimate of both can place a moving point on that chart. It is useful for exploration, but it is not a direct measurement of tongue position.

Hold a steady vowel — ee, ah, oo — for about a second. Your position appears as a moving dot against the reference vowels. Audio is analysed in your browser and never uploaded or stored.
